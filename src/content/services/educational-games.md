An educational game only works if it survives two very different audiences: students who will
abandon anything boring within thirty seconds, and teachers who need evidence that time spent
playing produced learning.

I design the mechanic around the learning objective so that getting better at the game
requires genuinely understanding the material — rather than bolting quiz questions onto an
unrelated game loop. Feedback is immediate and specific, because "wrong, try again" teaches
nothing.

Practical constraints shape everything else: classroom devices are often old, networks are
often throttled, and headphones are often unavailable. Every game I build assumes all three.
