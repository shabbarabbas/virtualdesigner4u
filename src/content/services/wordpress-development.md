WordPress powers a huge share of the web, and most of it is slower and more fragile than it
needs to be. The usual culprit is a stack of overlapping plugins and a commercial theme trying
to be everything for everyone.

I take the opposite approach. Your theme is written for your content model, your editors get
fields that match how they actually think about their pages, and functionality is added in
code rather than by installing another plugin with its own update cycle and attack surface.

Output is escaped, input is sanitised, queries are prepared, and capabilities are checked —
the fundamentals that keep a public-facing WordPress site out of trouble.
